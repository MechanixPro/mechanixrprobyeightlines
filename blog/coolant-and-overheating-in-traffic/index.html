Coolant and overheating in slow traffic

10 November 2026 · Mechanix Pro

Long waits at signals and crawling traffic do not give your engine much airflow. Coolant and the cooling fan have to do the work.

Which bikes use coolant

Many newer bikes and some scooters are liquid-cooled. Others are air-cooled or oil-cooled. Your owner's manual says which you have, and what coolant to use.

If your bike is liquid-cooled, the coolant level and condition matter all year, not only in summer.

Air-cooled engines rely on airflow and their fins to lose heat, so they can also run hot in slow traffic, even though they have no coolant to check.

Warning signs of overheating

  • The temperature gauge or warning light climbs
  • The engine loses power or pings
  • You smell hot coolant or see steam
  • The cooling fan runs constantly or not at all

Pull over safely if you see any of these.

If the fan never seems to come on in traffic, or runs and the temperature still climbs, mention both to your mechanic. These are useful clues.

What to do if it overheats

Switch off the engine in a safe spot and let it cool. Never open the radiator or coolant cap while the engine is hot, because hot coolant can spray out under pressure.

Do not pour cold water onto a hot engine. Once it is cool, check for leaks and the coolant level, and call for help if you are not sure.

Keeping the cooling system healthy

Check the level in the reservoir against the marks, when the engine is cold. Coolant ages, so follow the replacement guidance in your manual. Keep the radiator fins clear of mud and dust, and look at hoses for cracks or seepage.

Use the coolant type your maker specifies. Mixing types can cause problems.

Using plain water for long periods is not ideal, because coolant also protects against corrosion and boiling. Follow the maker's guidance on the mix.

Riding tips for hot days

Avoid revving hard while stuck in traffic, and switch off the engine at long waits if it is safe to do so. A bike that sits and idles in the sun gets hot quickly.

If you are on a long trip, take breaks and check the gauge now and then. Keep a close eye on the engine temperature if you are carrying a pillion or a heavy load.

Quick answers

Why does my bike overheat in traffic?

In slow traffic there is little airflow over the engine, so the cooling system works harder. Low coolant, a weak fan, a blocked radiator or old coolant can make it worse. A mechanic can check the cooling system.

Can I open the radiator cap when the engine is hot?

No. Hot coolant is under pressure and can spray out and cause burns. Switch off the engine, wait until it is fully cool, and then check the level. If you are unsure, ask a mechanic to do it.

How often should I change bike coolant?

Follow your owner's manual, which gives a time or distance interval and the type of coolant to use. Coolant ages even if the bike is not used much, so a periodic check is a good habit.
