Spark plug basics for riders

9 November 2026 · Mechanix Pro

The spark plug is a small part with a big job. Here is what it does and how to tell when it needs attention.

What a spark plug does

It creates the spark that ignites the fuel and air mix in the cylinder. If the spark is weak or late, the fuel burns poorly, and the engine runs rough.

Plugs wear over time because the gap between the electrodes slowly grows.

Some bikes have more than one plug per cylinder, and some use long-life plugs. Your manual tells you what applies to your model.

Signs of a tired plug

  • Hard starting, especially when cold
  • Misfiring or hesitation when you open the throttle
  • A rough or unsteady idle
  • Poor mileage or weak pickup

Other faults can look the same, so a check is wiser than guessing.

A plug that has been misfiring for a while can also wash fuel into the engine oil, which is another reason not to ignore a rough-running bike.

What the plug can tell you

A mechanic can read the colour and deposits on the plug tip. A light tan or greyish-brown tip is usually considered healthy. Black, sooty deposits can hint at a rich mix. Oily deposits can point to oil getting into the cylinder. A white, blistered tip can suggest overheating.

These are clues, not a diagnosis, so the cause still needs to be checked.

Use the right plug

Use the plug type and gap that your owner's manual lists. A wrong heat range or a loosely fitted plug can cause damage. Over-tightening can damage the threads in the cylinder head.

Replace plugs at the interval in your manual, or earlier if they look worn.

Never test a plug by touching it while the engine is running. The ignition system carries a high voltage that can give a nasty shock.

Why plugs foul

Short trips, long idling and a rich fuel mix are common reasons for a fouled plug in city riding. A weak battery, a dirty air filter or the wrong oil can also play a part.

If a new plug fouls quickly, the plug is probably not the root cause. Ask your mechanic to look at the air filter, the fuel mix and the engine condition, instead of just changing plugs again and again.

Quick answers

When should I replace my bike's spark plug?

Follow the interval in your owner's manual. Replace it sooner if the bike starts hard, misfires or loses mileage and the plug looks worn, sooty or oily. A mechanic can inspect it and advise whether cleaning or replacing makes sense.

Can a bad spark plug reduce mileage?

Yes. A weak or fouled plug can cause incomplete burning, so the engine wastes fuel and loses pickup. Other issues can cause the same symptoms, so have the plug and other basics checked by a mechanic.

What does a black spark plug mean?

Black sooty deposits often suggest a rich fuel mix, a dirty air filter or short, slow trips. It is a clue, not a final diagnosis. A mechanic can check the air filter, carburettor or injector and correct the cause.
