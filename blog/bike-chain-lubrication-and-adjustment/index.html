Chain lubrication and adjustment for your bike

2 November 2026 · Mechanix Pro

The chain carries every bit of power to your rear wheel, yet it is often forgotten. A few minutes of care keeps it quiet and helps it last.

Why the chain needs attention

A chain works in dust, grit and water. When it dries out or goes loose, it wears itself and the sprockets faster, and you may notice noise, jerks or a drop in smooth pickup.

Bengaluru's dust in summer and mud in the monsoon both pull oil away from the chain, so it needs checking more often than many riders expect.

A well-kept chain also makes the ride quieter and smoother. Many riders notice that once a noisy chain is cleaned and lubricated, the whole bike feels more relaxed.

Cleaning before you lubricate

Oiling a dirty chain just traps the grit. Wipe off old grease and mud with a rag, and use a chain cleaner or a soft brush if the dirt is thick. Avoid harsh solvents on chains with rubber seals, since they can damage the seals.

Let the chain dry for a short while before applying new lubricant.

How to lubricate it

Use a lubricant made for motorcycle chains. Apply a little to the inside run of the chain while turning the wheel slowly, then wipe off the extra so it does not fling onto the tyre or brake.

Keep lubricant away from the rear tyre and brake disc or drum at all times.

A good habit is to lubricate after the ride, not before, so the oil has time to settle into the links overnight. This keeps less of it flying off at speed.

Checking the slack

Your owner's manual gives the correct amount of chain play for your model. Check it with the bike on its stand, at the tightest point of the chain, because chains do not wear evenly.

A chain that is too tight strains the gearbox output and wheel bearings. One that is too loose can slip or jump off. If the chain is stretched, stiff in places or the sprockets look hooked, it is usually time to replace the set together.

Take a look at the rear sprocket teeth as well. Teeth that look sharp, hooked or uneven suggest the sprocket is worn, and a new chain on a worn sprocket will wear out quickly.

Chain types and sealed chains

Many bikes use an O-ring or X-ring chain, which has small rubber seals that keep grease inside. These last longer, but they still need cleaning and the right lubricant, and harsh cleaners can harm the seals.

Scooters often use a belt or a sealed chain case instead. If your scooter has a chain case, it may need oil or grease at intervals given in the manual, and a mechanic can check that.

Quick answers

How often should I lubricate my bike chain?

Many riders do it every couple of weeks, and again after riding in rain or heavy dust. Your owner's manual may suggest a different interval, so follow that. A dry, noisy or rusty-looking chain needs attention sooner.

How do I know if my bike chain is too loose?

If the chain sags more than the play stated in your owner's manual, slaps against the swingarm, or makes jerks when you accelerate, it is likely too loose. Measure on the stand at the tightest point, or ask a mechanic.

Can I use engine oil on my chain?

It is not recommended. Engine oil flings off quickly and does not cling like chain lubricant. Use a lubricant made for motorcycle chains, and wipe off any extra so it stays off the tyre and brakes.
