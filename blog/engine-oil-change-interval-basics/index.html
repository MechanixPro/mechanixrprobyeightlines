Engine oil change: interval basics for riders

3 November 2026 · Mechanix Pro

Fresh oil is the cheapest protection your engine gets. The right interval depends on your bike, so here is how to work it out.

What engine oil does

Oil lubricates moving parts, carries heat away and holds tiny particles in suspension so they do not scratch the engine. Over time it breaks down and gets loaded with dirt, and it stops doing these jobs well.

In many bikes it also lubricates the clutch and gearbox, so old oil can show up as clutch or gear trouble.

Oil does not only wear out by distance. Even a bike that is ridden very little can have oil that ages with time, which is why many manuals give a time limit as well as a distance.

Finding your interval

Your owner's manual or service booklet lists the oil change interval, usually by distance or time, whichever comes first. Many commuter bikes ask for a change every few thousand kilometres or every few months.

If you ride short trips in heavy traffic, the engine seldom gets fully warm and oil can age faster than the kilometre count suggests.

A good way to remember is to write the date and odometer reading on a small sticker or in your phone after each change. That way you always know when the next one is due.

Using the right oil

Use the grade and specification given in the manual. Thicker is not better, and mixing random oils can affect the clutch and fuel economy. Bikes and scooters can need different oils, so do not assume one fits all.

Synthetic, semi-synthetic and mineral oils all exist. Choose the type your maker allows, and ask your mechanic what is going in.

Signs oil is overdue

  • Oil on the dipstick or sight glass looks very dark or gritty
  • The level is low between changes
  • The engine sounds rougher or hotter than usual
  • Gear changes feel notchy

A leak under the bike or smoke from the exhaust is not an interval issue. Get it inspected.

Checking the level yourself

Many bikes have a dipstick or a small window on the engine. Check the level with the bike upright on level ground, and follow the manual on whether the engine should be warm or cold for the reading.

Do not overfill. Too much oil can cause frothing, leaks and a rough-running engine. If the level drops often between changes, mention it to your mechanic, because it can point to a leak or burning oil.

Please do not drain oil onto the ground or into a drain. Used oil is harmful to the environment, and it should be handed over for proper disposal.

Quick answers

How often should I change engine oil in my bike?

Follow the interval in your owner's manual, usually by kilometres or months, whichever comes first. Many commuter bikes ask for a change every few thousand kilometres. Heavy city traffic can age oil faster, so change it sooner if it looks dark or low.

Does a scooter need the same oil as a bike?

Not always. Scooters and bikes can need different grades and specifications, especially where the clutch shares oil with the engine. Use exactly what your owner's manual specifies, and ask your mechanic to confirm before pouring.

What happens if I never change engine oil?

Old oil loses its ability to lubricate and cool, so parts wear faster and the engine can run hotter and rougher. Over time this can lead to expensive damage. Regular oil changes are one of the simplest ways to protect your engine.
