Puncture: tube vs tubeless and what to do on the road

5 November 2026 · Mechanix Pro

A puncture can happen to any rider. Knowing what kind of tyre you have, and what to do next, keeps a bad moment from getting worse.

Tube tyres and tubeless tyres

A tube-type tyre has an inner tube that holds the air. When it is punctured, air usually escapes fast and the tyre goes flat quickly. A tubeless tyre holds air directly against the rim and often loses air more slowly when a small object is stuck in it.

Your tyre sidewall and owner's manual tell you which type your bike uses. Do not assume.

Some bikes with spoked wheels use tube-type tyres, while many bikes with alloy wheels use tubeless tyres. This is a general pattern, not a rule, so check your own bike.

Signs of a puncture

  • The bike feels heavy or wobbly at the rear or front
  • Steering feels vague
  • You hear a flapping sound
  • One tyre looks lower than the other

Slow down gently. Do not brake hard or turn sharply.

Check your tyres by eye before every ride. Many punctures are found early by a quick look at the tread and a press of the tyre with your thumb.

What to do on the road

Pull over somewhere safe, away from traffic, and switch on your hazard lights if you have them. Do not ride far on a flat tyre, because it can damage the rim and the tyre itself and make steering unsafe.

If a nail or object is stuck in a tubeless tyre, leave it in place. It may be sealing the hole. Call for help rather than pulling it out.

Repair or replace

Small punctures in the tread area can often be repaired. Sidewall damage, large cuts, bulges or a tyre that has been ridden flat should not be repaired and need a professional opinion.

Tyres are a safety part, so have the repair checked by a mechanic.

Do not use quick-fix sprays or sealants without asking a mechanic. They can hide a problem and may not suit your tyre type.

Carrying the right basics

A small portable inflator can help with a slow leak. If your bike has tubeless tyres, a basic plug kit is something some riders carry, though it is only a temporary fix and the tyre should be properly inspected soon after.

Whatever you carry, know how to use it before you need it. If you are not confident, it is safer to call for help than to attempt a roadside repair in traffic.

Quick answers

What is the difference between tube and tubeless tyres?

A tube tyre uses an inner tube to hold air, so a puncture usually deflates it quickly. A tubeless tyre seals air against the rim, so small punctures often leak slowly. Check your owner's manual to know which type your bike has.

Should I remove the nail from my tubeless tyre?

No. Leave it in. It may be plugging the hole and slowing the air loss. Ride no further than necessary, or better still, stop and call for help so the tyre can be checked and repaired properly.

Can I ride a bike with a flat tyre to a repair shop?

Avoid it. Riding on a flat tyre can damage the tyre and rim, and makes steering unsafe. It is better to stop somewhere safe and get help to come to you or arrange a pickup.
